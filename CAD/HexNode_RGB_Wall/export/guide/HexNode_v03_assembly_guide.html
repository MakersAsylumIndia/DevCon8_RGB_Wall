Tools

  • Soldering iron and solder for the power wires and the contact pads
  • Wire cutter and stripper to cut the JST cable wires and trim the filament
  • Scissors for the copper tape and painter's tape
2

1Copper tape on the spring tabs

  • bracket
  • 2 × copper tape 10 × 30 mm

Cut two strips of 10 mm wide copper tape, each 30 mm long. Peel off half the backing and stick that half on a spring tab, on the magnet side of the bracket. Push the rest of the strip through to the other side and stick it down firmly all round. On the other side, the tab has a groove for the power wire: press the tape into the groove. Do the same on the second tab, so both +5V and GND are taped.

3

2Power wires

  • JST cable no. 1
  • wire stripper
  • soldering iron

Take JST cable no. 1. Cut two of its wires to 60 mm: red and brown (on a red / brown / black cable) or red and black (on a red / black / yellow cable). Don't cut the third wire (black or yellow): it becomes the touch wire later. Strip about 10 mm off the cut ends and solder them to the copper tape in the tab grooves: red to the + tab, the other wire to the − tab.

4

3Solder the contact pads

  • solder

Turn the bracket over. Each spring tab has a raised pad at its end: this is what touches the power rail on the wall. Run a thin layer of solder over the copper tape on each pad.

5

4Anchor the wires

  • painter's tape

Lay each wire in its groove on the bracket and hold it down with a long strip of painter's tape: from the spring tab (over the copper tape) all the way to the edge of the bracket.

6

5Carrier base onto the bracket

  • carrier base

Put the carrier base on the bracket. The [+] and [−] marks on the bracket and on the carrier base must line up.

7

6Power connector into its socket

  • JST connector no. 1

Slide JST connector no. 1 (the power cable) into its socket on the carrier base, on the side with the [−] mark (the one labelled PWR). The red wire goes nearest the edge of the carrier base.

8

7LED ring connector into its socket

  • JST connector no. 2 (on the LED ring)

The LED ring already has a 3-pin JST cable soldered to it. Slide this JST connector no. 2 into the other socket, on the side with the [+] mark (labelled LED). Again, the red wire goes nearest the edge.

9

8Fit the LED ring

  • RGB LED ring

Fit the LED ring into the carrier base. Turn it so its wires are at about 10 to 11 o'clock, near the LED socket (USB end of the node = 12 o'clock). Two of the holes in the ring should now line up with two of the four clips that hold it.

10

9Tape the wires down

  • painter's tape

Check that the wires from both connectors run in the cable channels. Put a narrow strip of painter's tape across the top edge of the carrier base, over the wires, so they stay in place.

11

10Filament pins in the deck

  • carrier deck
  • 3 × filament 1.75 mm, 30 mm long

Push three 30 mm pieces of 1.75 mm 3D-printer filament through the three holes in the carrier deck. Make the filament flush with the top face of the deck.

12

11Deck onto the stack

  • deck from the step before

Fit the deck, passing the filament pins through the matching holes in the carrier base and the bracket. Two keying tabs only let it go on one way: the wide end of the deck at 6 o'clock, the small USB cut-out at 12 o'clock. Once it is lined up, push it all the way in. The three printed parts are now held together as one sandwich.

13

12Trim the filament

  • wire cutter

Turn the node over. The three filament pins stick out about 10 mm from the magnet side of the bracket. Cut them flush with the bottom of the bracket, so the node sits flat on the wall.

14

13ESP32

  • ESP32 DevKit

Fit the ESP32 with its USB connector at 12 o'clock. The black plastic strips of the header pins must go all the way into the long slots in the deck.

15

14Touch washer

  • metal washer
  • 1 mm double-sided foam tape
  • painter's tape

Stick a piece of 1 mm double-sided foam tape on the ESP32's metal shield. Cut the last wire of JST cable no. 1 (the touch wire, black or yellow) to about 110 mm and strip 20–25 mm off the end. Twist the bare strands and fix them firmly to the metal washer. Cover any bare wire with painter's tape, but leave the washer uncovered. Stick the washer on the foam tape.

16

15Magnets

  • 2 × magnet 10 × 10 × 2 mm

Press two 10 × 10 × 2 mm neodymium magnets into the pockets under the bracket. There are four pockets, but two are enough. Put both magnets in the same way round (same pole facing out).

If it doesn't work

What you seeCauseFix
Nothing lights up on the wallThe contact pads don't touch the rails, or a power wire is looseSteps 2–3: check the solders and add a little more solder on the pads
LEDs dark but the ESP32 is onLED connector (no. 2) the wrong way roundStep 7: red wire nearest the edge
ESP32 doesn't power upPower connector (no. 1) in the wrong socket or the wrong way roundStep 6: [−] / PWR socket, red wire nearest the edge
Touch does nothingWasher covered by tape, or the wire is loose on the washerStep 14: bare washer, wire twisted on firmly
Node won't sit flat on the wallFilament sticking out under the bracket, or a magnet standing proudStep 12: trim the filament flush; press the magnets fully into their pockets
Parts don't line upCarrier base turned roundStep 5: [+] over [+], [−] over [−]
18

On the wall

Press the node onto its place on the RGB Node Wall: the magnets hold it, and the soldered pads on the spring tabs pick up +5V and GND from the copper rails. Enjoy!

The node is fully parametric. The printed parts, the laser-cut rails and the pictures in this guide come from one FreeCAD script, and the firmware is in the same repository.
